## How Records Are Verified

To maintain integrity, all Hall of Fame entries must pass the following verification process:

### Step 1: Benchmark Validation

Submit a screenshot or validation link from an approved benchmark platform:

| Platform | Accepted Evidence | Link |
|----------|-------------------|------|
| **HWBot** | Submission link with verified score | [hwbot.org](https://hwbot.org) |
| **CPU-Z** | Validation link (.cvf file or online validator) | [valid.x86.fr](https://valid.x86.fr) |
| **AIDA64** | Benchmark report with system info | Built-in report export |
| **PassMark** | Online result link | [passmark.com](https://www.passmark.com) |
| **3DMark** | Online result link (for combined scores) | [3dmark.com](https://www.3dmark.com) |

### Step 2: Hardware Verification

- Photo of the VOLTX module(s) used, clearly showing the label with model and serial number
- Photo of the full system showing cooling setup
- Screenshot of CPU-Z SPD tab showing module details

### Step 3: TwinMOS Review

Our team reviews submissions within 14 business days:
- Verify benchmark authenticity against public databases
- Confirm hardware identification
- Check for rule compliance (no cheating, no modified benchmarks)

### Step 4: Publication

Approved records are published on this page with full attribution. Top records are featured in TwinMOS marketing and social media.

## Submit a Record

Think you have what it takes to make the Hall of Fame? Submit your validated score for review.

### Submission Requirements

1. **Benchmark Evidence:** Link or screenshot from an approved platform (see above)
2. **Hardware Photos:** Clear images of your VOLTX modules and full system
3. **System Specifications:** CPU, motherboard, BIOS version, cooling method, ambient temperature
4. **Overclocker Profile:** Name, country, team affiliation (if any), social handles
5. **Settings Summary:** Voltage, timings, frequency, and any special tuning applied

### Rules

- Only TwinMOS / VOLTX products are eligible for VOLTX-specific records
- Submissions must be your own work — no proxy overclocking
- Extreme cooling (LN2, LHe, DICE) is allowed and encouraged for frequency records
- Daily-driver cooling (air, AIO, custom loop) has a separate category
- Any form of benchmark manipulation results in permanent disqualification

## Tips from the Pros

### Getting Started with Extreme Overclocking

1. **Start with XMP/EXPO:** Master your daily overclock before moving to extreme cooling
2. **Know Your IMC:** Every CPU's memory controller (IMC) is different. Learn your chip's limits
3. **Voltage Scaling:** DDR5 voltage scaling is non-linear. Small increments (0.01V) yield better results than large jumps
4. **Temperature Matters:** Colder memory often scales higher. LN2 is the gold standard for frequency records
5. **Timing Tuning:** Primary timings (tCL, tRCD, tRP, tRAS) give the biggest gains. Tertiary timings fine-tune benchmark scores
6. **Stability vs. Validation:** Validation screenshots don't require full stability — but daily overclocks do

### Recommended Tools

| Tool | Purpose | Download |
|------|---------|----------|
| **CPU-Z** | Frequency and timing validation | [cpuid.com](https://www.cpuid.com) |
| **HWBot XOC Statistics** | Compare scores globally | [hwbot.org](https://hwbot.org) |
| **AIDA64 Extreme** | Memory bandwidth and latency testing | [aida64.com](https://www.aida64.com) |
| **TestMem5** | Stability testing with custom configs | Community forums |
| **Thaiphoon Burner** | SPD reading and analysis | [thaiphoonburner.com](https://www.thaiphoonburner.com) |
