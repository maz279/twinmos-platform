## The 2025–2026 DRAM Supply Landscape

Understanding the market context is essential for procurement planning:

- **AI workloads are consuming approximately 20% of global DRAM wafer capacity in 2026**, as hyperscalers compete for HBM and high-bandwidth modules
- **Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron** are increasingly prioritizing large hyperscalers — leaving smaller OEMs exposed to spot market volatility
- **DDR4 production has ended** at major fabs; final DDR4 module availability is tightening throughout 2026
- **DRAM contract prices rose 55–60% quarter-over-quarter in Q1 2026** for spot buyers without allocation agreements
- **Crucial (Micron's consumer brand) exited the retail consumer memory and SSD market in early 2026**, creating a significant gap in mainstream module availability for resellers and system builders

**TwinMOS OEM partners benefit from structured allocation agreements**, protecting against spot market surges and ensuring BOM cost predictability across project cycles.

## Platform for the DDR6 Transition

DDR6 platform sampling and validation is expected to begin in late 2026, with mass adoption projected in 2027. For system builders evaluating platform investment decisions:

- **DDR5 is the correct platform investment now** — Intel and AMD AM5 platforms will support DDR5 through at least 2027
- **DDR4 is approaching end-of-life** — new motherboard platforms for consumer desktops are exclusively DDR5 from 2025 onward
- **TwinMOS will provide DDR6 product roadmap information** to registered OEM partners ahead of general market availability
