## The 2025–2026 Gaming Performance Reality

The PC gaming meta has shifted decisively to DDR5 in 2025. DDR4 DRAM chip production from major manufacturers ended by mid-2025, and DDR5-6000MHz has established itself as the definitive performance sweet spot for both Intel and AMD platforms:

- **DDR5-6000 delivers 9–14% more FPS** than DDR5-5600 in CPU-bound gaming scenarios on AMD Ryzen 7000/9000 platforms
- **32GB (2×16GB) at 6000MHz** is the 2025 standard for gaming rigs — 16GB is no longer sufficient for modern open-world titles
- **Enabling XMP (Intel) or EXPO (AMD) in BIOS** is mandatory — without it, your DDR5-6000 kit runs at the JEDEC default of 4800MHz, leaving up to 14% performance untapped
- NVMe SSDs reduce open-world micro-stutters by **10–20% versus SATA** SSDs, thanks to faster random read delivery of streaming assets

TwinMOS VOLTX DDR5 and CoreX/Xtreme NVMe are built specifically around these real-world demands.

## Common Gaming Mistakes — and How to Fix Them

**Problem: RAM running at 2133 or 4800MHz despite purchasing DDR5-6000**
→ Solution: Enable XMP 3.0 (Intel) or AMD EXPO in BIOS. This activates your rated speed profile with one click.

**Problem: Micro-stutters in open-world games**
→ Solution: Ensure 32GB installed. Many 2025 titles exceed 16GB memory footprint when game + OS + background apps are loaded simultaneously.

**Problem: Long load times in games with large asset packs**
→ Solution: Upgrade from SATA to NVMe. NVMe SSDs reduce open-world stutter by 10–20% due to faster random read performance.

**Problem: RGB lighting not syncing with motherboard**
→ Solution: Install the motherboard manufacturer's lighting software (Aura Sync / RGB Fusion / Mystic Light / Polychrome Sync) and enable DRAM RGB synchronization in settings.
