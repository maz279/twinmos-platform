## 1. Purpose and Scope

This guide provides cultural adaptation guidelines for TwinMOS website content in each active locale. It is distinct from the Translation Glossary (which covers terminology) and the Tone of Voice Guide (which covers language register). Cultural adaptation addresses:

- **Register and formality** — how formal or informal the language should feel
- **Imagery and visual content** — what works culturally; what to avoid
- **Colour and symbolism** — colours with cultural significance or negative connotations
- **Business relationships** — how TwinMOS's local presence, partners, and certifications should be featured
- **Calendar and campaigns** — regional events and peak shopping seasons
- **Currency and pricing** — how pricing should be displayed
- **Compliance and certification** — region-specific marks that build credibility
- **Tone cautions** — topics, phrasings, or claims to handle carefully in each region

This guide is mandatory reading for:
- Translation vendors working on TwinMOS content
- Marketing team members creating locale-specific campaigns
- Content authors localising product descriptions or landing pages
- UX/UI designers adapting imagery or visual layouts per region

## 2. UAE / GCC — Arabic (AR)

### 2.1 Market Context

TwinMOS's headquarters are in Dubai Airport Free Zone (DAFZA), UAE. The Gulf Cooperation Council (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, Oman) is TwinMOS's most strategically important Arabic-speaking market. B2B relationships with distributors and retailers are central to sales in this region. The UAE itself is a highly cosmopolitan, tech-forward market with a significant expat population.

### 2.2 Language Register

- Use **Modern Standard Arabic (MSA)** for all web content. Gulf Colloquial Arabic (Khaleeji dialect) is not appropriate for a brand website.
- Register should be **formal-to-neutral professional** — more formal than the EN content tone, but not stiff or bureaucratic.
- Avoid overly casual expressions, slang, or Western idioms translated literally.
- Use gender-neutral language where possible. Arabic grammar requires gendered verb agreement; opt for forms that address a general "user" (المستخدم) rather than assuming gender.
- Honorifics: In B2B partnership pages, use appropriate professional address. In consumer product pages, a warm but professional tone is correct.

### 2.3 Imagery and Visual Content

- **Avoid** imagery showing mixed-gender casual social scenarios (e.g., young men and women relaxing together informally in a home setting) — such images can be culturally inappropriate in conservative GCC contexts.
- **Prefer** professional technology settings: workstations, server rooms, gaming setups (single user), product-only renders, abstract tech imagery.
- **Acceptable**: Images of diverse professionals in office settings, IT teams, gaming enthusiasts in gaming chairs.
- For gaming/VOLTX content specifically: a solo gamer at a high-end RGB gaming setup is universally accepted and aspirational.
- Arabic calligraphy or geometric/arabesque design elements can be used sparingly as decorative accents, but should not be overused to the point of stereotyping.
- People in imagery should be dressed modestly — avoid exposed skin beyond what is standard in corporate/tech contexts.

### 2.4 Colour and Symbolism

| Colour | Significance | Usage Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Green | Very positive — associated with Islam, nature, prosperity | Appropriate for success states, positive CTAs, sustainability messaging |
| White | Purity, cleanliness | Positive; common in product backgrounds |
| Blue | Trust, professionalism | Strong choice for corporate content |
| Red | Caution, can imply danger | Use carefully in UI (errors OK; marketing use cautiously) |
| Yellow/Gold | Prosperity, premium | Good for premium product highlighting |
| Black | Sophistication, luxury | Appropriate for premium/gaming product lines |
| Purple | Royalty, luxury | Acceptable |

Avoid combinations with strong religious symbolism unless intentional (e.g., green-and-white combination has national flag resonance for Saudi Arabia; keep product design neutral).

### 2.5 Local Presence and Partnerships

- **Always mention TwinMOS's Dubai HQ** on relevant pages — it is a significant trust signal for GCC buyers.
- Reference DAFZA (Dubai Airport Free Zone) on the About and Contact pages.
- If specific GCC distribution partners are named, confirm they are current and accurate before publishing.
- **Where to Buy** page for the AR locale should prioritise UAE and GCC regional retailers.
- Local customer support contact (if available for UAE) should be prominently featured.

### 2.6 Campaigns and Calendar

| Event/Season | Relevance | Content Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ramadan | March–April (lunar calendar) | Muted promotion during fasting hours; Eid al-Fitr sale opportunity; avoid imagery of food/drink |
| Eid al-Adha | June–July (lunar calendar) | Gift-giving season; family tech purchases; significant campaign opportunity |
| GITEX Technology Week | October, Dubai | Major regional tech trade show; align press releases and product launches |
| National Day (UAE) | 2 December | Flag-adjacent content; patriotic themes acceptable for UAE-specific pages |
| White Friday | Late November | GCC equivalent of Black Friday; major e-commerce sale event |
| Back to School | September | Education-focused content; laptop RAM upgrades |

### 2.7 Currency and Pricing

- Primary currency: **AED (د.إ.)** for UAE
- Secondary: **SAR (ر.س)** for Saudi Arabia pages (if regional landing pages created)
- Pricing on product pages: Show AED price only if TwinMOS has confirmed AED pricing; otherwise link to "Where to Buy" for local pricing.
- Price points in the GCC are generally higher than India or Bangladesh — messaging can lean into quality and premium positioning.

### 2.8 Compliance and Certifications

- **CE, RoHS** marks are recognised and valued in UAE (following European standards).
- **REACH** compliance is relevant for health-conscious buyers.
- Mention TwinMOS's ISO 9001:2015 certification prominently — quality certification is a strong purchase driver in B2B GCC procurement.
- UAE consumer protection regulations: Ensure product claims are accurate and substantiated.

### 2.9 Tone Cautions

- Avoid political references to specific regional conflicts or geopolitical topics entirely.
- Do not use the word "Palestine/Israel" or any territorial claim language in product or marketing copy.
- Do not reference alcohol, pork, gambling, or other Islamically prohibited items even in comparative or metaphorical language.
- "الحلال" (halal) as a quality descriptor is irrelevant for electronics — do not use it in a tech marketing context.

## 3. India — Hindi (HI)

### 3.1 Market Context


### 3.2 Language Register

- Hindi for the HI locale is aimed at **mass-market Indian consumers**. B2B and enterprise buyers in India often prefer English — Hindi content is for consumer acquisition and regional search rankings.
- Register: **warm, confident, and aspirational** — avoid both over-formal and overly casual.
- Hindi web content frequently mixes English technical terms within Hindi sentences (Hinglish). This is natural and acceptable for product specifications, but avoid excessive Hinglish in marketing copy.
- Avoid translating technical terms that are universally known in English in India (e.g., "laptop", "RAM", "gaming", "WiFi" — these are used in everyday Hindi and do not need translation).

### 3.3 Imagery and Visual Content

- Indian diversity is important: avoid imagery that represents only one regional or ethnic type.
- Gaming content: young, aspirational Indian gamers (both male and female where contextually appropriate) resonate well.
- Family tech scenarios (student studying, professional working from home) are effective for consumer memory and storage products.
- Festival imagery (Diwali lights, Holi colours) is appropriate for seasonal campaigns but should not appear on evergreen product pages.
- Avoid imagery that is overtly Western-lifestyle-centric (e.g., American kitchen, European Christmas) for content targeting the Indian market.

### 3.4 Colour and Symbolism

| Colour | Significance | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Saffron/Orange | Sacred, auspicious (Hinduism) | Use with care in product design; fine in UI accents |
| Green | Auspicious, prosperity, Islam | Positive |
| White | Mourning in Hindu context | Avoid as sole UI colour in celebration contexts |
| Red | Auspicious, celebrations | Positive — good for CTA and sale badges |
| Yellow/Gold | Prosperity, Diwali | Very positive for festive campaigns |
| Blue | Krishna iconography; trust | Positive for tech |
| Black | Mourning, negative in some contexts | Use sparingly in marketing copy |

### 3.5 Local Presence and Partnerships

- TwinMOS's **New Delhi office** is a trust signal — feature the address and contact on the contact and about pages.
- **Local customer support**: If a Hindi-language support email or phone number is available for India, feature it prominently on the Hindi support pages.

### 3.6 Campaigns and Calendar

| Event/Season | Relevance | Content Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Diwali | October–November | Biggest shopping season in India; gift buying; major campaign opportunity |
| Holi | March | Colour-themed content opportunity; less commercial than Diwali |
| Republic Day | 26 January | National pride; acceptable for Indian regional content |
| Independence Day | 15 August | National pride; patriotic themes |
| Back to School / College | June–July | Student memory and SSD upgrades |
| Big Billion Days / Great Indian Festival | October | Flipkart/Amazon India peak sales; align content to shopping period |
| IPL Season | March–May | Cricket-season gaming content angle (gaming during IPL) |

### 3.7 Currency and Pricing

- Currency: **INR (₹)**
- India has significant price sensitivity — messaging should balance aspirational quality claims with value messaging.
- Emphasise "best value for performance" and "best-in-segment" claims with substantiation.
- BIS certification is mandatory for electronic products sold in India — feature it prominently. Absence of BIS certification would be a significant red flag for Indian buyers.

### 3.8 Compliance and Certifications

- **BIS (Bureau of Indian Standards)** — mandatory and should be featured prominently on India product pages and the compliance page.
- **India DPDP Act 2023** — Privacy Policy must reference the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 for Indian users.

### 3.9 Tone Cautions

- Avoid political commentary on India-Pakistan relations or other sensitive geopolitical topics.
- Caste-related language or references should be entirely absent.
- Religious diversity in India is vast — do not reference any specific religion positively or negatively in product marketing.

## 4. Bangladesh — Bengali (BN)

### 4.1 Market Context

Bangladesh is a key South Asian market for TwinMOS, with a Dhaka office providing on-the-ground support. The market is predominantly mobile-first, with a price-sensitive, value-conscious consumer base. The IT market is concentrated in Dhaka (Elephant Road is the major retail electronics hub). Growing gaming and student demographics are the primary consumer segments for TwinMOS products.

### 4.2 Language Register

- Bengali (Standard Bangladeshi Bangla) — not Indian Bengali.
- Register: **friendly, clear, and accessible**. Avoid highly formal or literary Bengali.
- Technical terms in English are widely used in Bangladesh tech contexts — do not over-translate terms that English-educated Bangladeshi consumers use daily.
- Mobile-first audience means content should be concise — avoid long paragraphs.

### 4.3 Imagery and Visual Content

- Urban Bangladeshi youth (especially Dhaka-based) are the primary audience — relatable imagery.
- Avoid imagery that is overtly Indian (Indian celebrities, Indian cultural references) — Bangladeshi national identity is distinct.
- Gaming setups, students studying, home office scenarios are all appropriate.
- Modesty in imagery is culturally appropriate — Bangladesh is a Muslim-majority country.

### 4.4 Colour and Symbolism

- **Green and Red** — colours of the Bangladeshi national flag. Using these together in specific arrangements could create unintentional flag associations. Use individually without issue.
- **Green** — positive, associated with Islam and nature.
- **Red** — the liberation movement; patriotic.
- General colour guidance similar to GCC (Muslim-majority cultural context).

### 4.5 Local Presence and Partnerships

- TwinMOS's **Dhaka office** is a key trust signal — feature the address, local contact number, and support team on the contact and regional pages.
- If local Bangladeshi distribution partners are confirmed, feature them on the Where to Buy page for Bangladesh.

### 4.6 Campaigns and Calendar

| Event/Season | Relevance |
|---|---|
| Eid al-Fitr | Major gifting and shopping season; most important commercial period |
| Eid al-Adha | Secondary shopping period |
| Pohela Boishakh (Bengali New Year) | 14 April; cultural significance; appropriate for brand messaging |
| Victory Day | 16 December; national pride |
| International Mother Language Day | 21 February; celebrate Bengali language and culture |

### 4.7 Currency and Pricing

- Currency: **BDT (৳)**
- High price sensitivity — emphasise durability, value, and local warranty service.
- Entry-level and mid-range products are more relevant for Bangladesh than premium lines.

### 4.8 Compliance

- Products sold in Bangladesh should reference local import compliance where applicable.
- Privacy policy localisation for Bangladesh: No equivalent to GDPR; reference general data protection principles.

## 5. Russia and CIS — Russian (RU)

### 5.1 Market Context

Russia and the CIS (Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Belarus, Azerbaijan, Armenia, Georgia) represent a significant distribution market for TwinMOS. The Russian market has a technically sophisticated consumer base that values performance metrics and benchmark data.

### 5.2 Language Register

- **Formal Russian** — web content in Russia/CIS is generally more formal than in English-speaking markets.
- Avoid abbreviations in body copy — Russian readers prefer words written out in full.
- Technical precision is valued — support claims with specific numbers, benchmark results, and spec comparisons.
- Avoid Western marketing clichés translated directly into Russian — they sound hollow in the Russian cultural context.

### 5.3 Imagery and Visual Content

- Neutral, professional, international imagery is appropriate.
- Avoid imagery that could be perceived as political (flags of specific countries, maps of contested regions).
- Dark-themed, high-tech, RGB gaming imagery resonates well with the Russian gaming community.

### 5.4 Geopolitical Sensitivity

- Do **not** include maps that show disputed territories.
- Do **not** reference political events, sanctions, or relationships between countries.
- Country-specific content should focus purely on product availability, distribution partners, and technical support.
- Do **not** mention Belarus in a way that implies political association with any geopolitical bloc.
- The CIS region is treated as a single entity for website purposes — avoid country-by-country political sensitivity.

### 5.5 Local Presence and Partnerships

- Feature EAC (Eurasian Conformity) certification prominently — it is legally required for goods sold in the Eurasian Economic Union (Russia, Kazakhstan, Belarus, Armenia, Kyrgyzstan) and is a strong trust signal.
- If TwinMOS has CIS distribution partners, feature them on the Where to Buy page for the Russian locale.

### 5.6 Campaigns and Calendar

| Event/Season | Relevance |
|---|---|
| New Year (1 January) | Biggest shopping season in Russia; gift-giving |
| Defender of the Fatherland Day | 23 February; gifting occasion for men |
| International Women's Day | 8 March; gifting occasion |
| Victory Day | 9 May; patriotic; no commercial tie-in |
| Back to School | August–September |

### 5.7 Currency and Pricing

- Currency: **RUB (₽)** for Russia; **KZT (₸)** for Kazakhstan pages (if separate landing pages created).
- Do not display USD or EUR pricing for Russian-market content — this creates friction and suggests prices may change with exchange rates.

### 5.8 Compliance

- **EAC (Eurasian Conformity)** certification is mandatory for most electronic goods sold in the Eurasian Economic Union. Feature prominently.
- **Federal Law 152-FZ** (Russian data protection law) requires Privacy Policy reference for Russian users.
- **Roskomnadzor** (Russian internet regulator): Ensure cookie consent and data processing disclosures comply with Russian law.

## 6. China — Chinese Simplified (ZH-CN)

### 6.1 Market Context

China's PC hardware market is enormous and technically sophisticated. Consumers are highly price-performance conscious and rely heavily on reviews, benchmarks, and peer recommendations via Chinese social platforms (Zhihu, Bilibili, Weibo). The market is also heavily influenced by distributors and e-commerce platforms (JD.com, Tmall, Taobao).

### 6.2 Language Register

- **Standard Simplified Chinese** — Mandarin as used in Mainland China.
- Technical Chinese is direct and factual. Marketing copy should emphasise performance metrics, value, and specifications.
- Avoid overly literal translations of English marketing slogans — they often lose meaning. Adapt the concept rather than translating word-for-word.
- Chinese consumers appreciate social proof — if TwinMOS has media reviews or awards, cite them in Chinese-language content.

### 6.3 Imagery and Visual Content

- Red and gold are very positive colours — associated with luck, prosperity, and celebration.
- Use clean, minimal, technically focused imagery — Chinese tech buyers respond to premium product renders.
- Avoid white backgrounds for gifting occasions (white is associated with mourning).
- Gaming content: high-performance PC gaming setup imagery resonates strongly.

### 6.4 Colour and Symbolism

| Colour | Significance | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Red | Very positive — luck, celebration, prosperity | Use freely in CTAs and promotional content |
| Gold/Yellow | Prosperity, premium | Excellent for premium product lines |
| White | Mourning in some contexts | Fine for product backgrounds; avoid as sole design colour in celebratory content |
| Green | Positive | Fine for success states, environmental claims |
| Blue | Trust, technology | Strong choice for corporate content |
| Black | Sophistication, luxury | Good for premium/gaming |
| Purple | Luxury | Fine |

### 6.5 Search Engine Considerations (Baidu)

The ZH-CN locale serves mainland China, where Google is blocked. Baidu is the dominant search engine. Baidu has different ranking signals from Google:

- **Baidu prefers pages hosted in China** (ICP Beian registration required). If TwinMOS plans serious China SEO, a China-hosted version may be necessary — this is beyond the current scope but noted here.
- **Baidu indexes meta keywords** — add a Chinese `<meta name="keywords">` tag (unlike Google, Baidu still uses this).
- **Baidu Open Graph**: Baidu uses its own social sharing tags — add `<meta property="baidu_share_image">` for content shared via Baidu.
- **Baidu Baike** — if TwinMOS has a Baidu Baike (Wikipedia equivalent) page, ensure it is accurate.

For the current project scope, the ZH-CN sitemap and hreflang will be implemented, but deep Baidu optimisation is out of scope for Phase 3.

### 6.6 Social and Content Platforms

- **Weibo** — Chinese Twitter equivalent. If TwinMOS has a Weibo presence, link to it on ZH-CN pages.
- **Bilibili** — Dominant video platform for tech reviews. If TwinMOS has Bilibili content, link to it.
- **WeChat** — Messaging + mini-program platform. A WeChat mini-program or official account would be Phase 4+ scope.
- Do not link to YouTube, Facebook, Twitter/X, Instagram, or Google services on ZH-CN pages — these are blocked in China and links will confuse Chinese users.

### 6.7 Local Presence

- If TwinMOS products are sold through JD.com, Tmall, or other Chinese e-commerce platforms, link to these on the Where to Buy page for ZH-CN.
- China distribution partner details on the regional China page.

### 6.8 Campaigns and Calendar

| Event/Season | Relevance |
|---|---|
| Chinese New Year | January–February; very significant; gifting and self-purchase |
| 618 Shopping Festival | 18 June; JD.com's major sale event |
| Double Eleven (光棍节) | 11 November; China's biggest shopping day |
| Double Twelve (双十二) | 12 December; secondary major sale event |
| National Holiday (Golden Week) | 1–7 October |
| Back to School | September |

### 6.9 Currency

- Currency: **CNY (¥)**
- Chinese consumers expect CNY pricing — do not display USD.

### 6.10 Compliance

- **GB Standards** — Chinese national standards for electronic products. Confirm TwinMOS products comply with relevant GB standards.
- **PIPL (Personal Information Protection Law)** — China's personal data law. Privacy Policy must reference PIPL for Chinese users.
- **ICP Beian** — If serving pages from a China-hosted domain or CDN, ICP registration is required. For the current project (Cloudflare Pages, non-China hosting), this is not required but noted for future planning.

## 7. Francophone Markets — French (FR)

### 7.1 Market Context

The French locale serves two quite distinct audiences:
1. **European French-speaking markets**: France, Belgium, Luxembourg, Switzerland
2. **Francophone African markets**: Morocco, Tunisia, Algeria, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire, Cameroon, Democratic Republic of Congo

TwinMOS has distribution partners in North Africa and Sub-Saharan Africa. The European French market is a secondary priority after the Francophone African opportunity.

### 7.2 Language Register

- **Standard French (français standard)** — comprehensible to both European and African Francophone speakers.
- Avoid Canadian French (Québécois) terms — not the target market.
- Register: **professional, clear, and technically credible**. French readers value grammatical precision and clear, well-structured sentences. Avoid marketing clichés.
- Vous form throughout (formal "you") — never tu in a brand-to-customer context.

### 7.3 Regional Splits

For marketing and campaign purposes, North African Francophone markets (Morocco, Tunisia, Algeria) may require slightly different:
- Currency (MAD, TND, DZD rather than EUR)
- Regional distributor references
- Seasonal content (Ramadan/Eid applies here as for Arabic-speaking GCC)

For Phase 3, a single FR locale is used. Regional landing pages for specific Francophone African countries would be Phase 4+ scope.

### 7.4 Imagery

- Neutral, professional, international imagery appropriate for both European and African Francophone contexts.
- Tech enthusiasts, professionals, students — diverse and globally relatable.
- Avoid imagery that appears exclusively European or exclusively African — the unified FR locale serves both.

### 7.5 Colour and Symbolism

- Standard Western colour associations apply for European French.
- Francophone African contexts: bright colours are culturally positive and energetic — avoid overly muted palettes for that audience.
- French tricolore (bleu-blanc-rouge) should not be used in TwinMOS brand materials — it would inappropriately associate TwinMOS with France's national identity.

### 7.6 Local Presence and Partnerships

- If TwinMOS has North African distribution partners, feature them in the Where to Buy section for the FR locale.
- For European France, if there are French or Belgian distributors or retailers, feature them.

### 7.7 Campaigns and Calendar

| Event/Season | Relevance |
|---|---|
| Soldes (French Sales) | January and July; major retail discount periods in France |
| Fête des Mères / Fête des Pères | May/June; Mother's Day/Father's Day in France |
| La Rentrée (Back to School) | September; major shopping season in France |
| Ramadan/Eid | Relevant for North African Francophone markets |
| Black Friday | Growing in France and Francophone Africa |

### 7.8 Currency and Pricing

- **EUR (€)** for European French markets
- **MAD (درهم)** for Morocco; **TND** for Tunisia; **DZD** for Algeria — for regional pages if created
- Note: Commas are used as decimal separators in French (1 234,56 €, not 1,234.56 €)

### 7.9 Compliance

- **RGPD (Règlement Général sur la Protection des Données)** — the French-language term for GDPR. Use "RGPD" in French content when referencing the regulation.
- **CNIL (Commission Nationale de l'Informatique et des Libertés)** — the French data protection authority. Reference CNIL in French Privacy Policy if applicable.
- **CE marking** and compliance are particularly trusted in the French market.
- **DMA (Loi pour la confiance en l'économie numérique)** — French digital services law; cookie consent must comply.

## 8. Phase 4 Notes — Spanish, Portuguese, German

These locales are out of the current project engagement scope (Phase 4, Month 16+). Brief adaptation notes are documented here for future reference.

### 8.1 Spanish — Latin American (ES)

- Use **Latin American Spanish** (not Castilian) — broader geographic reach.
- Formal Usted form for product and corporate pages; informal tú may be acceptable for gaming/VOLTX content (confirm with regional contact).
- Key markets: Mexico, Colombia, Argentina, Chile, Peru.
- Currency: Varies by country (MXN, COP, ARS, CLP, PEN) — Where to Buy approach recommended over displayed prices.
- Campaign peaks: Hot Sale (Mexico, May), Cyber Monday/Black Friday, Buen Fin (Mexico, November).
- Avoid references to political or economic instability in specific countries.

### 8.2 Portuguese — Brazilian (PT)

- Use **Brazilian Portuguese** (not European Portuguese).
- Register: warmer and more informal than European Portuguese; Portuguese consumers expect directness.
- Key market: Brazil (secondary: Portugal).
- Currency: BRL (R$) for Brazil; EUR for Portugal.
- Campaign peaks: Black Friday (biggest in Brazil), Carnaval (indirect, brand-awareness), back-to-school.
- LGPD (Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados) — Brazil's data protection law; Privacy Policy must reference LGPD.

### 8.3 German — DACH (DE)

- **Very formal register** — German B2B and consumer tech buyers expect technical precision and formal language.
- Duzen (informal "du") vs. Siezen (formal "Sie"): Use **Sie** throughout.
- Germans value accuracy, substantiation, and technical detail — avoid marketing hyperbole.
- **Impressum** (legal notice) is legally mandatory for German-language websites — the /de/ locale must have an Impressum page.
- **DSGVO (Datenschutz-Grundverordnung)** — German term for GDPR. Use in German Privacy Policy.
- Imagery: Clean, precise, professional. Avoid overt lifestyle imagery that feels aspirational without substance.
- Currency: EUR (€) for Germany, Austria; CHF for Switzerland.
- Campaign peaks: Weihnachten (Christmas) — biggest gifting season; Black Friday growing.

## 9. Cross-Regional Principles

### 9.1 Universal Cultural Dos

- **Do** highlight TwinMOS's global presence and ISO certification to build trust in all markets.
- **Do** adapt currency, certification, and distributor references for each region.
- **Do** use region-appropriate date and number formats (see Locale QA Checklist).
- **Do** feature local office or support contact where it exists (UAE, India, Bangladesh).
- **Do** acknowledge major regional shopping events with appropriate promotional content.

### 9.2 Universal Cultural Don'ts

- **Don't** use political, religious, or geopolitical references in product marketing.
- **Don't** make claims that cannot be substantiated in specific regional contexts (e.g., a "fastest in the market" claim may be true globally but false in a specific regional market).
- **Don't** use imagery of people that ignores regional diversity and representation.
- **Don't** translate Western idioms literally — adapt the concept, not the words.
- **Don't** assume that content well-received in the English-speaking market will translate directly to another cultural context.
- **Don't** publish locale content without native-speaker review, regardless of translation quality.

### 9.3 Escalation Process for Cultural Sensitivity Issues

If a translator, reviewer, or TwinMOS team member identifies content that may be culturally inappropriate for a specific locale:

1. Flag the issue immediately to the **Localization Lead** with:
   - Exact content in question
   - Target locale
   - Nature of the concern (imagery, language, cultural reference)
   - Suggested alternative
2. Localization Lead reviews within **24 hours** and escalates to the regional office contact if needed.
3. The Marketing Director makes the final call on any content change.
4. The change is documented and the original content flagged as "Locale: Do Not Use" in the CMS.
