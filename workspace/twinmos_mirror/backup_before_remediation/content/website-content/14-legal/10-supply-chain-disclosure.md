# Supply Chain Disclosure

> **Version 2.0 — Last Reviewed 2026-04-30**

TwinMOS Technologies is committed to ethical sourcing, environmental responsibility, human rights protection, and transparency throughout its supply chain. This disclosure describes our approach to supply chain due diligence, the practices we expect from our suppliers, and our alignment with applicable legislation and international standards.

## 1. Our Supply Chain Overview

TwinMOS Technologies designs, sources, and distributes memory and storage solutions globally. Our supply chain encompasses:

- **Raw material extraction:** Silicon, copper, gold, tantalum, tin, tungsten, and specialty materials used in semiconductor and PCB manufacturing
- **Component manufacturing:** NAND flash memory, DRAM chips, SSD controllers, PCBs, connectors, and packaging materials — primarily sourced from qualified suppliers in Taiwan, Japan, South Korea, and China
- **Final assembly and quality assurance:** Memory modules and SSDs assembled at qualified manufacturing facilities in Taiwan; USB and portable drives assembled in Taiwan and Southeast Asia
- **Distribution:** TwinMOS's regional distribution hubs in Dubai (UAE), New Delhi (India), Dhaka (Bangladesh), Lagos (Nigeria), and Johannesburg (South Africa)

We work with over 100 component suppliers and contract manufacturers across our global supply chain.

## 2. Due Diligence Framework

### 2.1 Supplier Assessment & Qualification
- All new suppliers undergo a qualification process that includes assessment of financial stability, quality management systems, compliance with applicable laws, and environmental and social standards
- New suppliers are required to complete a **Supplier Questionnaire** covering labor practices, environmental compliance, conflict minerals sourcing, anti-corruption policies, and health and safety
- High-risk suppliers (based on sector, geography, or value of relationship) receive enhanced due diligence, including third-party audits

### 2.2 Ongoing Monitoring
- Annual self-assessment questionnaires distributed to Tier 1 suppliers
- Periodic on-site audits conducted by TwinMOS procurement staff or third-party auditors for strategic and high-risk suppliers
- Continuous monitoring of supplier compliance via industry databases and news sources
- Risk re-assessment triggered by changes in supplier ownership, significant non-conformance, or changes in supply chain geography

### 2.3 Corrective Action & Remediation
- Non-compliances identified through audits or questionnaires result in documented Corrective Action Plans (CAPs) with defined timelines
- Critical non-compliances (e.g., identified instances of forced labor, child labor, or gross environmental violations) trigger immediate escalation to senior management
- Persistent non-compliance after CAP period may result in disqualification of the supplier

## 3. Key Due Diligence Practices

| Area | Practice |
|---|---|
| **Labor Standards** | Supplier compliance with ILO Core Conventions; prohibition of forced labor, child labor, and human trafficking; fair wages and working hours |
| **Health & Safety** | Safe working conditions, PPE provision, fire safety, emergency procedures aligned with ILO guidelines and national law |
| **Environment** | RoHS and REACH compliance; monitoring of hazardous substances; waste management; emissions reduction goals |
| **Conflict Minerals** | 3TG (tin, tantalum, tungsten, gold) due diligence per OECD guidelines and EU Conflict Minerals Regulation 2017/821 |
| **Anti-Corruption** | Zero tolerance for bribery and corruption; supplier commitment to anti-corruption laws including FCPA and UK Bribery Act 2010 |
| **Subcontracting** | Suppliers must disclose and obtain approval for significant subcontracting; subcontractors subject to equivalent standards |

## 4. EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD)

The **EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (2024/1760)** (CSDDD) entered into force on 25 July 2024 and requires member states to implement it by 26 July 2027. Large EU companies (and non-EU companies with significant EU turnover) will be required from 2028 to conduct comprehensive human rights and environmental due diligence across their value chains.

TwinMOS is proactively preparing for the CSDDD by:
- Conducting supply chain mapping to identify human rights and environmental risks at all tiers
- Enhancing our supplier audit programs and corrective action processes
- Developing stakeholder grievance mechanisms accessible to affected communities and workers
- Preparing a climate transition plan aligned with the Paris Agreement 1.5°C pathway
- Documenting due diligence activities in anticipation of reporting obligations

We will provide CSDDD-aligned disclosures as required once the directive is transposed into applicable national law.

## 5. EU Forced Labour Regulation

The **EU Forced Labour Regulation (2024/3015)** entered into force on 13 December 2024, with the product prohibition becoming effective from **14 December 2027**. The Regulation prohibits the placing, making available, or exporting of products made with forced labor on the EU market, covering the entire supply chain.

TwinMOS's preparatory actions include:
- Supply chain mapping of high-risk materials (cobalt, rare earth elements, tantalum, tin, tungsten, and gold from conflict-affected or high-risk areas)
- Verification of no-forced-labor certifications from strategic suppliers
- Development of specific audit protocols for forced labor indicators (recruitment fees, passport confiscation, restricted movement, etc.)
- Engagement with industry initiatives and multi-stakeholder programs to address systemic risks
- Supplier contract provisions prohibiting forced labor and granting audit rights
