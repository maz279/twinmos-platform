# Vendor Code of Conduct

> **Version 2.0 — Last Reviewed 2026-04-30**

TwinMOS Technologies expects all vendors, suppliers, subcontractors, and service providers (collectively, "Suppliers") to maintain high ethical, social, and environmental standards throughout their operations and supply chains. This Code of Conduct ("Code") sets out the minimum standards that all TwinMOS Suppliers are required to meet.

This Code is aligned with the **Responsible Business Alliance (RBA) Code of Conduct Version 8.0** (effective January 2024), which is grounded in international frameworks including the **UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights**, the **ILO Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work**, the **OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises**, and the **UN Global Compact**.

By accepting a purchase order, entering into a supply agreement, or otherwise conducting business with TwinMOS, Suppliers agree to comply with this Code and to require compliance from their own sub-suppliers.

---

## 1. Labor & Human Rights

### 1.1 Freely Chosen Employment
- Slavery, human trafficking, bonded labor, indentured labor, debt bondage, forced labor (including prison labor, involuntary labor, and involuntary overtime), and compulsory labor are strictly prohibited
- Employment must be freely chosen; workers must be free to leave their employer after reasonable notice without penalty
- Suppliers must not use physical or psychological coercion, threats, confiscation of identity documents, restriction of movement, or other means to retain workers involuntarily
- Recruitment agencies used by the Supplier must operate ethically; workers must not be charged recruitment fees

### 1.2 Child Labor & Young Workers
- The employment of child labor is strictly prohibited. **Child labor** is defined as work by any person under the minimum working age established by local law, or under 15 years of age (whichever is higher), or under 14 in countries where ILO Convention 138 exemptions apply
- Young workers (age 15 to 18) must not perform hazardous work, night shifts, or overtime; their working hours and conditions must comply with local law and ILO Convention 138 and 182
- Suppliers must conduct due diligence on the age of workers at hiring

### 1.3 Working Hours
- Working hours must comply with applicable laws and industry standards
- Workers should not be required to work more than 60 hours per week (including overtime) on a regular basis, or the local legal limit, whichever is less
- Workers must receive at least one day off per seven consecutive days
- Overtime must be voluntary and compensated at the appropriate premium rate

### 1.4 Wages & Benefits
- Suppliers must pay at least the legal minimum wage or the industry benchmark wage, whichever is higher
- Workers must receive legally mandated benefits (social insurance, paid leave, etc.)
- Wages must be paid on time, in full, and in the currency specified; deductions not authorized by law or collective agreement are prohibited
- Wage information must be provided clearly and in a language the worker understands

### 1.5 Humane Treatment
- Workers must be treated with dignity and respect
- Harsh and inhumane treatment, including physical punishment, sexual harassment, psychological or verbal abuse, or threats of such treatment, are strictly prohibited
- Disciplinary processes must be fair, documented, and free from coercion

### 1.6 Non-Discrimination & Equal Opportunity
- Suppliers must not discriminate in hiring, compensation, training, promotion, or disciplinary practices on the basis of race, color, age, gender, sexual orientation, gender identity, ethnicity, religion, disability, pregnancy, political opinion, national origin, or union membership
- Employment decisions must be based on merit, qualifications, and performance

### 1.7 Freedom of Association & Collective Bargaining
- Suppliers must respect workers' rights to form and join trade unions or worker representative bodies of their choosing, bargain collectively, and engage in lawful collective action, in accordance with applicable local law
- Where local law restricts these rights, Suppliers must allow parallel means for workers to organize and communicate their concerns
- Workers who exercise these rights must not be subject to retaliation, dismissal, or discrimination

## 2. Health & Safety

### 2.1 Safe Working Conditions
- Suppliers must provide a safe and hygienic working environment that minimizes the risk of accidents, injuries, and occupational illness
- Safety procedures, emergency exits, firefighting equipment, and first-aid facilities must be in place, regularly maintained, and clearly communicated to workers
- Hazardous materials must be identified, safely handled, and stored with appropriate safety data sheets

### 2.2 Training & Communication
- Workers must receive appropriate safety training in their own language
- Safety-related information must be posted visibly in work areas
- Workers must be able to report safety concerns without fear of retaliation

### 2.3 Emergency Preparedness
- Emergency response plans must be documented, communicated, and regularly practiced
- Emergency exits must be unobstructed and clearly marked

### 2.4 Occupational Health
- Suppliers must have programs to identify, assess, and minimize occupational health hazards (chemical exposure, noise, ergonomic risks, etc.)
- Workers exposed to hazardous conditions must receive appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) at no charge

### 2.5 Worker Accommodation
- Where Suppliers provide housing for workers, it must be safe, clean, and meet minimum living standards with access to sanitation and potable water

---

## 3. Environmental Responsibility

### 3.1 Legal Compliance
- Suppliers must comply with all applicable environmental laws, regulations, and permit requirements in the jurisdictions where they operate

### 3.2 Restricted Substances
- Suppliers must comply with applicable restrictions on hazardous substances, including the EU RoHS Directive, REACH Regulation, and equivalent national requirements
- Components supplied to TwinMOS must meet applicable substance restrictions, and suppliers must provide compliant material declarations upon request

### 3.3 Waste & Emissions Management
- Suppliers must implement programs to minimize waste, air emissions, water usage, and other environmental impacts
- Hazardous waste must be identified, managed, and disposed of in accordance with applicable law
- Recyclable materials should be recovered and recycled where economically and technically feasible

### 3.4 Climate
- Suppliers are encouraged to set greenhouse gas reduction targets and implement energy efficiency measures aligned with the goals of the Paris Agreement

---

## 4. Business Ethics

### 4.1 Anti-Corruption & Bribery
- Bribery, corruption, extortion, embezzlement, and facilitation payments are strictly prohibited in all forms
- Suppliers must comply with all applicable anti-bribery and anti-corruption laws, including the UK Bribery Act 2010, the US Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA), and applicable local laws
- Suppliers must not offer, promise, give, authorize, solicit, or accept anything of value to improperly influence a business decision or government action

### 4.2 Conflict of Interest
- Suppliers must identify and disclose to TwinMOS any actual or potential conflict of interest in the business relationship
- Gifts, hospitality, and entertainment provided to TwinMOS employees must be modest, infrequent, and within applicable legal and policy limits

### 4.3 Intellectual Property & Confidentiality
- Suppliers must respect intellectual property rights and handle TwinMOS confidential information and trade secrets with appropriate care
- TwinMOS's designs, product specifications, process data, and other proprietary information may not be disclosed or used for any purpose other than fulfilling the supply relationship without TwinMOS's prior written consent

### 4.4 Fair Dealing & Competition
- Suppliers must conduct business in a fair and transparent manner and comply with applicable competition and antitrust laws

### 4.5 Accurate Records & Disclosure
- Suppliers must maintain accurate business records, including financial accounts, quality records, and compliance documentation
- Suppliers must provide TwinMOS with complete, accurate, and timely information as required under the supply agreement

---

## 5. Management Systems

### 5.1 Commitment & Accountability
- Supplier senior management must demonstrate a commitment to compliance with this Code
- A designated individual must be responsible for compliance with this Code

### 5.2 Legal & Customer Requirements
- Suppliers must identify and comply with all applicable legal requirements and TwinMOS's contractual requirements relating to the supply of goods and services

### 5.3 Risk Assessment & Continuous Improvement
- Suppliers must periodically assess risks related to labor, health and safety, environment, and business ethics in their own operations and supply chains
- Suppliers must implement improvement plans to address identified risks and track progress against measurable objectives

### 5.4 Training
- Relevant supplier personnel must be trained on the requirements of this Code

### 5.5 Grievance Mechanisms
- Suppliers must provide workers and other stakeholders with accessible, confidential channels to raise concerns about working conditions, ethics, or compliance with this Code without fear of retaliation
- Suppliers must investigate concerns raised through grievance mechanisms and take appropriate corrective action

### 5.6 Sub-Supplier Requirements
- Suppliers must communicate and require compliance with the principles of this Code from their own sub-suppliers, particularly for high-risk categories

## 6. Conflict Minerals

Suppliers providing components containing tin, tantalum, tungsten, or gold (3TG) must:
- Complete the **Conflict Minerals Reporting Template (CMRT)** annually upon TwinMOS's request
- Exercise supply chain due diligence consistent with OECD guidelines and the EU Conflict Minerals Regulation (2017/821)
- Preferentially source from smelters and refiners certified by the Responsible Minerals Initiative (RMAP) or equivalent recognized programs

Refer to TwinMOS's [Conflict Minerals Policy](/legal/conflict-minerals/) for details.

## 7. Compliance, Audits & Consequences

TwinMOS reserves the right to:
- Request documentation of compliance with this Code at any time
- Conduct announced or unannounced audits of Supplier facilities and records
- Require third-party social compliance audits at Supplier's expense for high-risk suppliers or where non-compliance is identified

Violations of this Code may result in:
- Issuance of a formal corrective action request with defined remediation timeline
- Suspension or restriction of the supply relationship pending remediation
- Termination of the supply agreement for serious or unaddressed violations
- Legal action where violations constitute criminal conduct or breach of contract

Critical violations — including confirmed use of forced labor, child labor, or human trafficking — will result in immediate suspension and potential termination of the business relationship.
