# FCC Compliance Statement

> **Version 2.0 — Last Reviewed 2026-04-30**

TwinMOS products marketed and sold in the United States comply with **Title 47, Code of Federal Regulations, Part 15** (FCC Part 15) rules for unintentional and incidental radiators, as applicable to the product type.

## FCC Part 15 — Class B Digital Device Declaration

Unless otherwise specified on the product label, TwinMOS computing peripherals, memory modules, and solid-state drives are classified as **Class B digital devices** per FCC Part 15. Class B devices are subject to stricter emission limits than Class A devices, as they are marketed for use in residential environments where closer proximity to radio and television receivers is expected.

This equipment has been tested and found to comply with the limits for a Class B digital device, pursuant to **Part 15 of the FCC Rules**.

**These limits are designed to provide reasonable protection against harmful interference in a residential installation.** This equipment generates, uses, and can radiate radio frequency energy and, if not installed and used in accordance with the instructions, may cause harmful interference to radio communications. However, there is no guarantee that interference will not occur in a particular installation.

If this equipment does cause harmful interference to radio or television reception (which can be determined by turning the equipment off and on), the user is encouraged to try to correct the interference by one or more of the following measures:
- Reorient or relocate the receiving antenna
- Increase the separation between the equipment and receiver
- Connect the equipment into an outlet on a circuit different from that to which the receiver is connected
- Consult the dealer or an experienced radio/TV technician for help

## Measurement Standard Update (Effective October 30, 2025)

Per FCC rule revisions effective **October 30, 2025**, measurements for intentional radiators (§ 15.31(a)(3)) must be performed using test procedures specified in **ANSI C63.10-2020** (American National Standard of Procedures for Compliance Testing of Unlicensed Wireless Devices). All TwinMOS products requiring FCC intentional radiator testing are tested per this updated measurement standard.

## Modifications

**Caution:** Changes or modifications to this equipment not expressly approved by TwinMOS Technologies could void the user's authority to operate the equipment under the FCC Part 15 rules.

## FCC ID

Products that require an FCC ID (equipment authorization) bear the FCC ID on the product label. The FCC ID can be used to look up the product's authorization details at the FCC Equipment Authorization Database (fccid.io or search at fcc.gov/oet/ea).

For products classified as unintentional radiators that do not require an individual FCC ID, compliance is achieved through a Supplier's Declaration of Conformity (SDoC).
