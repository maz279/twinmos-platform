# Modern Slavery Statement

> **Version 2.0 — Financial Year Ended 31 December 2025**

This statement is made pursuant to **Section 54 of the UK Modern Slavery Act 2015** and covers TwinMOS Technologies' financial year ending 31 December 2025. It sets out the steps we have taken during the year to ensure that modern slavery and human trafficking are not occurring in our business operations or supply chains.

This statement has been approved by the Board of Directors of **TwinMOS Technologies Middle East FZE**.

---

## 1. Organization Structure & Business

**TwinMOS Technologies** is a global designer, manufacturer, and distributor of memory and storage solutions, including DRAM modules, NVMe and SATA solid-state drives, portable storage devices, and USB flash drives. Founded in 1998 and headquartered in Taipei, Taiwan, we have operations in the UAE, Taiwan, India, Bangladesh, Nigeria, and South Africa, and sell products in over 93 countries through a network of 500+ distribution partners.

**Group entities relevant to this statement:**

| Entity | Jurisdiction |

| TwinMOS Technologies Bangladesh Ltd. | Dhaka, Bangladesh |

Our supply chain encompasses raw material suppliers, semiconductor manufacturers, contract assembly partners, component distributors, and logistics providers operating primarily in Taiwan, Japan, South Korea, China, and Southeast Asia.

## 2. Policies Addressing Modern Slavery

TwinMOS maintains the following policies relevant to modern slavery and human trafficking:

- **[Vendor Code of Conduct](/legal/vendor-code-of-conduct/)** — Mandatory ethical standards for all suppliers, including explicit prohibitions on forced labor, child labor, debt bondage, and human trafficking
- **[Supply Chain Disclosure](/legal/supply-chain-disclosure/)** — Our approach to supply chain due diligence, including supplier assessments, audits, and corrective action processes
- **[Conflict Minerals Policy](/legal/conflict-minerals/)** — Due diligence on tin, tantalum, tungsten, and gold to avoid sourcing from conflict-affected areas that may involve forced or coerced labor
- **Internal Employment Policy** — Prohibiting the use of forced, compulsory, or bonded labor in our own operations; ensuring employees are paid at or above minimum wage with freely chosen employment
- **Whistleblower / Grievance Policy** — Confidential reporting channels for employees, workers, and third parties to report suspected violations without fear of retaliation

## 3. Risk Assessment

### 3.1 Risk Identification
We identify modern slavery risks through:
- Geographic and sector risk mapping (using tools such as the Global Slavery Index)
- Annual supplier self-assessment questionnaires
- Third-party audit results
- Industry intelligence and NGO reports

### 3.2 High-Risk Areas Identified

| Risk Factor | Description |
|---|---|
| **Migrant labor in manufacturing** | Contract manufacturing facilities in Southeast Asia may employ migrant workers who are vulnerable to recruitment fraud and debt bondage |
| **Raw materials extraction** | Minerals such as tantalum, tin, tungsten, and gold are sourced from regions (including parts of Africa and Southeast Asia) where artisanal mining may involve forced or child labor |
| **Tier 2+ suppliers** | Visibility decreases beyond Tier 1 suppliers; sub-suppliers may operate in jurisdictions with weaker labor law enforcement |
| **Distribution hubs** | Warehouse and logistics workers in certain operating regions may be at elevated risk of labor exploitation |

### 3.3 Risk Mitigation
- Priority auditing focused on higher-risk supplier tiers and geographies
- Enhanced questionnaires for suppliers in elevated-risk sectors and countries
- Contractual clauses requiring suppliers to cascade modern slavery standards to their own sub-suppliers

---

## 4. Due Diligence Processes

### 4.1 Supplier Screening & Onboarding
- All new Tier 1 suppliers must complete our **Supplier Ethics Questionnaire** covering labor practices, living wages, working hours, forced labor indicators, and grievance mechanisms
- Suppliers in high-risk categories are required to provide third-party audit reports (e.g., RBA CSMS audit, SA8000 certification, or equivalent)
- Unacceptable audit findings are addressed through mandatory Corrective Action Plans (CAPs) with defined timelines; failure to remediate results in disqualification

### 4.2 Ongoing Monitoring
- Annual self-assessment questionnaire re-submission for all strategic Tier 1 suppliers
- Periodic on-site audits (announced and unannounced) for high-risk suppliers
- Monitoring of news, NGO reports, and industry databases for emerging risks
- Post-CAP follow-up audits to verify remediation

### 4.3 Contractual Protections
All supplier contracts include:
- Explicit prohibition of forced labor, child labor, debt bondage, human trafficking, and compulsory overtime
- Right to audit the supplier and its sub-suppliers with reasonable notice
- Requirement to flow down modern slavery standards to sub-suppliers
- Termination rights for confirmed violations without liability to TwinMOS

## 5. Training & Awareness

- **Procurement and supply chain staff** receive annual training on modern slavery risk identification, supplier assessment, and escalation procedures
- **HR and management staff** receive training on identifying signs of forced labor and human trafficking in recruitment and employment practices
- **New employee induction** includes awareness of modern slavery indicators and reporting obligations
- **Supplier awareness:** Modern slavery standards and expectations are communicated to suppliers through our Vendor Code of Conduct, onboarding process, and periodic communications

## 7. Actions Taken in FY2025

During the financial year ended 31 December 2025, TwinMOS took the following actions to address modern slavery risks:

- Updated our Vendor Code of Conduct to align with the **Responsible Business Alliance (RBA) Code of Conduct v8.0** (effective January 2024)
- Commenced preparatory work for compliance with the **EU Forced Labour Regulation (2024/3015)** (product ban effective December 2027)
- Engaged with our key contract manufacturing partners in Taiwan to verify the absence of recruitment fees and debt bondage practices for migrant workers
- Extended our conflict minerals due diligence to align with the **EU Conflict Minerals Regulation (2017/821)**
- Reviewed and updated our internal whistleblower policy to ensure workers throughout our supply chain can report concerns confidentially

## 8. Looking Ahead — FY2026 Priorities
