# Data Center Solutions

## Powering Infrastructure at the Speed of Modern AI

The enterprise storage market reached **USD 47.52 billion in 2026** — driven by explosive demand from AI model training, inference serving, cloud expansion, and the accelerating mass migration from HDD to NVMe across enterprise infrastructure. **NVMe now accounts for over 72% of enterprise storage deployments**, and PCIe Gen5 is growing at over 30% year-over-year in new server shipments.

TwinMOS CoreX Pro Gen5 NVMe SSDs deliver 14,000 MB/s sequential read — engineered to meet the throughput demands of AI-adjacent storage, high-performance databases, virtualization hosts, and demanding edge cloud workloads.

---

## The Data Center Storage Inflection Point

### Why 2025–2026 Is the Turning Point

**NVMe vs HDD economics have crossed:**
- Average enterprise SSD pricing reached approximately USD 60/TB in 2025, making the total cost of ownership (TCO) equation favor SSDs for virtually all tier-1 and tier-2 workloads
- All-flash deployments reduce rack footprint by up to 90% versus equivalent HDD capacity — translating directly to lower power, cooling, and physical space costs per TB served
- **80% of new enterprise server infrastructure** deployed in the US in 2025 uses SSDs as primary storage

**PCIe Gen5 is the new server baseline:**
- PCIe Gen5 NVMe achieved a **40%+ attach rate** on new server hardware by mid-2025
- Performance-per-watt — not cost-per-GB — has become the primary data center procurement metric as energy costs dominate TCO calculations
- AI inference and training workloads are storage-sensitive, not just compute-sensitive: high-IOPS random read for vector databases, feature stores, and model checkpointing

## Edge Cloud & Distributed Infrastructure

The fastest-growing data center segment is not the hyperscale core — it is the **edge cloud**: distributed compute nodes deployed at the network edge, in colocation facilities, at cell tower sites, and in regional data centers closer to end users. These deployments require:

- **Compact form factor** (M.2 2280 fits single-socket 1U servers)
- **High sequential throughput** for media delivery, AI inference, and CDN workloads
- **Extreme reliability** — field support for edge nodes is expensive; hardware must not require frequent intervention
- **Wide operating temperature** in some deployments — particularly outdoor or infrastructure cabinet installations

CoreX Pro Gen5 and Xtreme Gen4 are both M.2 2280 form factor, fitting the compact single-socket platforms increasingly preferred for edge cloud deployments.
