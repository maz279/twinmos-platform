# Budget PC Build Guide: Maximum Performance for Under $800

Building a PC on a tight budget doesn't mean settling for a sluggish machine. With smart component selection, an $800 build can handle 1080p gaming at high settings, stream to Twitch, edit photos and video, and tackle everyday productivity with ease. This guide focuses on extracting maximum value from every dollar, with specific TwinMOS recommendations for memory and storage.

## Build Philosophy

Budget builds require prioritization. We allocate funds to components that deliver the most tangible performance benefit while saving on areas with diminishing returns.

**Priority Order:**
1. GPU (biggest gaming impact)
2. SSD (transforms system responsiveness)
3. CPU (sufficient for target workloads)
4. RAM (adequate capacity and speed)
5. Motherboard (features you actually need)
6. PSU (reliable but not overbuilt)
7. Case (functional airflow, not aesthetics)

## Recommended Build

### CPU: AMD Ryzen 5 5600 or Intel Core i5-12400F

Both offer 6 cores/12 threads — plenty for 1080p gaming and multitasking. The Ryzen 5600 works with affordable AM4 motherboards and DDR4. The Intel 12400F offers slightly better single-thread performance on a platform with upgrade paths to 13th/14th Gen.

If your budget allows a slight stretch (~$180-200 for the CPU), the AMD Ryzen 5 7600 on AM5 delivers notably better single-thread performance and a forward-looking DDR5 platform.

**Price**: ~$120-150

### Motherboard: B550 (AMD AM4) or B660 (Intel)

Budget chipsets provide the essentials: sufficient VRMs for stock CPUs, M.2 slots for NVMe SSDs, and adequate connectivity. Avoid premium features like PCIe Gen5 or Wi-Fi 6E that inflate cost without benefit at this tier.

**Price**: ~$90-110

### RAM: 16GB DDR4-3200

At this budget, DDR4 remains the smart choice. A 2×8GB kit of DDR4-3200 running in dual-channel mode provides sufficient capacity and bandwidth for 1080p gaming and productivity. Look for CAS latency CL16 for optimal value.

While TwinMOS specializes in DDR5, our recommendations align with platform needs — if building DDR4, select a reliable dual-channel kit from a reputable brand.

**Price**: ~$35-50

### Storage: TwinMOS Alpha Pro Gen3 500GB

An NVMe SSD is non-negotiable even in budget builds. The Alpha Pro Gen3 delivers up to 3,600 MB/s sequential read speeds — dramatically faster than any SATA drive and priced competitively. 500GB accommodates the OS, key applications, and 3-5 major games.

If budget allows, the 1TB model provides breathing room for more games and media.

**Price**: ~$40-50 (500GB), ~$65-75 (1TB)

### GPU: NVIDIA RTX 5060 or AMD Radeon RX 7600 XT

The GPU determines gaming performance more than any other component. Both options deliver strong 1080p performance at this budget:

- **NVIDIA RTX 5060** ($299): Blackwell architecture with DLSS 4 Multi Frame Generation support, excellent 1080p performance, and NVENC AV1 encoding for streaming
- **AMD Radeon RX 7600 XT** (~$249-279): Strong rasterization performance at 1080p with FSR 3.1 upscaling support, good for competitive gaming

Both handle modern games at high settings at 1080p. The RTX 5060 adds AI-powered upscaling advantages for supported titles; the RX 7600 XT often provides better raw rasterization per dollar.

**Price**: ~$250-300

### PSU: 550W 80+ Bronze

A reliable power supply protects your investment. 550W is sufficient for this build's power draw. Choose units from reputable manufacturers with actual safety certifications.

**Price**: ~$50-60

### Case: Budget ATX/Micro-ATX with Mesh Front

Airflow matters more than aesthetics at this price point. A case with a mesh front panel and support for 2-3 fans provides adequate cooling without premium pricing.

**Price**: ~$50-70

## Performance Expectations

### Gaming (1080p)
- Esports titles (Valorant, CS2, Rocket League): 144+ FPS on high settings
- AAA titles (Cyberpunk 2077, Black Myth: Wukong): 60+ FPS on high/medium settings
- With FSR/DLSS: 60+ FPS on ultra settings in supported titles

### Productivity
- Microsoft Office, web browsing: Instantaneous
- 1080p video editing: Smooth timeline scrubbing, reasonable export times
- Photo editing: Comfortable with 20+ layer PSDs
- Programming/development: Fast compilation for moderate projects

### Streaming
- 720p60 stream encoding via CPU (x264 fast preset) while gaming
- 1080p60 AV1 via GPU encoder (NVIDIA NVENC) with minimal performance impact on RTX 5060

## Upgrade Path

This build leaves room for strategic upgrades:

1. **Add storage**: Add a 1-2TB SATA SSD or second NVMe drive when budget allows
2. **More RAM**: Upgrade to 32GB (2×16GB) if you move into content creation or heavy multitasking
3. **Better GPU**: The PSU and CPU can handle up to an RTX 5060 Ti or RX 9070 class card
4. **Platform upgrade**: When the time comes, sell the AM4/LGA1700 system and build fresh on AM5 with DDR5

## Money-Saving Tips

- **Buy used GPUs**: A used RTX 4060 Ti or RX 7800 XT can fit this budget with significantly better performance than new entry-level cards
- **Skip the Windows license initially**: Use Windows unactivated (minor limitations) or consider Linux for non-gaming tasks
- **Reuse peripherals**: Monitor, keyboard, mouse, and speakers from an old system save $200+
- **Shop sales**: Black Friday, Prime Day, and seasonal sales can reduce component costs 10-30%

## Summary

A well-planned budget build punches above its weight. By prioritizing the GPU and SSD, accepting DDR4's continued viability at the AM4/LGA1700 tier, and choosing reliable components without premium frills, you can assemble a system that handles modern gaming and productivity comfortably for under $800.
